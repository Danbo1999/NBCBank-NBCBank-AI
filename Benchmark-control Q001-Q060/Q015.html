Trait Profile Report of Resveratrol

Question

Which traits are associated with resveratrol?

Executive Summary

This report details the traits associated with the functional component Resveratrol (FC03019) in Bos taurus (cattle). The analysis identified 163 distinct traits linked to resveratrol, supported by a total of 3,635 evidence records, 405 unique genes, and 346 annotation entries. The associated traits span seven major categories, with Milk Production Traits being the most prevalent (80 traits). The trait with the strongest association is Milk fat yield, supported by 235 records. Other highly supported traits include Milk myristoleic acid content (180 records) and Milk C14 index (175 records). The findings suggest resveratrol has a broad and significant influence on various economically important traits in cattle, particularly those related to milk production and composition.

Detailed Analysis

The trait profile reveals resveratrol's extensive and multi-faceted biological impact in cattle. The analysis is based on aggregated evidence linking genes and functional annotations to traits.

1. Trait Category Distribution
The 163 associated traits are distributed across the following primary categories:

  • Milk Production Traits (80 traits): This is the largest category, encompassing milk yield, composition (fat, protein, lactose), fatty acid profiles, udder health, and conformation. The high number of associations underscores resveratrol's potential significant role in mammary gland function and lactation biology.
  • Reproductive Performance (19 traits): Includes traits like calving ease, pregnancy rate, and age at puberty.
  • Growth & Conformation (18 traits): Includes body weight, stature, and body condition score.
  • Health & Disease (13 traits): Includes disease susceptibility and metabolic health indicators.
  • Carcass & Meat Quality (12 traits): Includes intramuscular fat, marbling, and carcass weight.
  • Feed Efficiency (8 traits): Includes residual feed intake and dry matter intake.
  • Comprehensive Performance (5 traits): Includes overall genetic merit indexes.
  • Other (8 traits): Includes miscellaneous traits.

2. Strength of Association
The association strength is quantified by three metrics:

  • Supporting Record Count (3,635): The total number of evidence entries linking resveratrol-associated genes/annotations to traits. This is the broadest measure of association.
  • Supporting Gene Count (405): The number of unique genes that are functionally linked to resveratrol and also associated with the listed traits.
  • Supporting Annotation Count (346): The number of unique functional annotations (e.g., GO, KEGG pathways) that connect resveratrol's mechanism to the traits.

The trait "/" (representing a general, non-specific association) has the highest supporting record count (2,140), indicating a vast base of general evidence. Among specific traits, Milk fat yield (PP01010164) stands out with the highest specific supporting record count (235), followed closely by traits related to specific milk fatty acids.

Conclusions and Recommendations

Conclusions:

  1. Broad-Spectrum Influence: Resveratrol is associated with a wide array of traits in cattle, indicating its potential as a multi-functional bioactive compound affecting lactation, reproduction, growth, health, and product quality.
  2. Strongest Link to Milk Traits: The most robust and numerous associations are with milk production and composition traits, particularly milk fat and protein yield/percentage. This suggests resveratrol may be a key candidate for modulating milk quality and quantity.
  3. Mechanistic Pathways: The high number of supporting genes (405) and annotations (346) implies that resveratrol's effects are mediated through complex biological networks and multiple molecular pathways.

Recommendations:

  1. Prioritize Research on Milk Fat Synthesis: Given the strong association with milk fat traits, further research should investigate resveratrol's specific mechanism in regulating lipid metabolism in the mammary gland. This could lead to nutritional strategies for enhancing milk fat content.
  2. Explore Health and Longevity Benefits: The associations with disease resistance (e.g., bovine respiratory disease) and productive lifespan warrant investigation into resveratrol's immunomodulatory and anti-aging effects in cattle, which could improve herd health and sustainability.
  3. Consider in Breeding & Nutrition Programs: The genetic links uncovered here suggest that polymorphisms in resveratrol-associated genes could be valuable markers for genomic selection. Simultaneously, dietary supplementation trials could validate its efficacy in improving key performance traits.
  4. Validate Specific Fatty Acid Profiles: The strong signal for specific milk fatty acids (e.g., myristoleic acid) presents an opportunity to tailor milk composition for specific human nutritional benefits through resveratrol intervention.
