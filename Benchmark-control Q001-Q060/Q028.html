Executive Summary

This report details the traits associated with the Growth Hormone Receptor (GHR) gene in cattle (Bos taurus). The analysis identified a total of 21 distinct traits linked to GHR, all supported by 5 evidence records each. These traits are predominantly related to milk production and composition, with additional associations found in reproduction, growth and carcass, feed efficiency, and longevity performance categories. The findings underscore the pivotal role of GHR in regulating key economic traits in dairy and beef cattle.

Detailed Analysis

The analysis reveals that GHR is a key regulator of multiple economically important traits in Bos taurus. The 21 associated traits can be categorized into several major functional groups:

  1. Milk Production and Quality (11 traits): This is the largest category, encompassing traits related to milk yield, fat and protein content, fatty acid profile, mineral content (calcium, magnesium, phosphorus), and processing properties (rennet coagulation time). The consistent evidence (5 records per trait) strongly supports GHR's central role in lactation biology.

  2. Reproduction (2 traits): GHR is associated with Dystocia (calving difficulty) and Calving to conception interval, indicating its influence on reproductive efficiency and calving ease.

  3. Growth and Carcass (3 traits): Associations with Intramuscular fat, Longissimus muscle area, and Subcutaneous fat thickness highlight GHR's significant impact on meat quality and carcass composition.

  4. Feed Efficiency (1 trait): The link to Residual feed intake suggests GHR's role in metabolic efficiency and nutrient partitioning.

  5. Longevity (1 trait): Association with Length of productive life points to GHR's broader influence on animal health and sustained productivity.

The uniform support record count of 5 for all traits indicates a consistent level of evidence strength across these associations, likely derived from multiple studies or data sources within the database.

Conclusion and Recommendations

Conclusion: The GHR gene is a major pleiotropic gene in cattle, with well-documented genetic associations spanning milk production, reproduction, growth, feed efficiency, and longevity. Its broad influence makes it a prime candidate for genetic selection and functional studies.

Recommendations:

  1. Breeding Program Focus: Given its strong association with milk component traits (fat and protein percentage), GHR should be prioritized in dairy cattle breeding programs aimed at improving milk quality.
  2. Balanced Selection: Breeders should be aware of potential genetic correlations. Selection for GHR variants favoring milk yield or composition might have correlated effects on reproduction (e.g., calving interval) or growth traits. A balanced breeding index is recommended.
  3. Functional Validation: The association with feed efficiency (Residual Feed Intake) warrants further investigation to understand the underlying metabolic mechanisms, which could lead to strategies for improving feed conversion.
  4. Cross-Species Consideration: The fundamental role of GHR in growth and metabolism suggests these findings may be relevant for genetic improvement in other ruminant species like goats (Capra hircus) and sheep (Ovis aries).
